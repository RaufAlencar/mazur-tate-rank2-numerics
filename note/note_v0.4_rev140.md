# Numerical verification of the refined Mazur–Tate conjecture in ranks 2 and 3, with an elementary formula for the Mazur–Tate pairing

Oct 21, 2026 · @Rauf Alencar

## Abstract

Let E/Q be an elliptic curve of Mordell–Weil rank r and, for a prime m of good reduction, let θ\_m ∈ Z\[(Z/m)ˣ/±1\] be the Mazur–Tate theta element built from plus modular symbols. The refined conjecture of Mazur and Tate (1987), in the formulation of Burns–Kurihara–Sano and Ota, predicts that θ\_m lies in the r-th power of the augmentation ideal I and prescribes its image in Iʳ/Iʳ⁺¹ as a Tamagawa-type factor times the discriminant of the Mazur–Tate height pairing. We report a numerical verification of this leading-term prediction, with both sides computed independently, for nine elliptic curves of rank 2 (389a1, 433a1, 446d1, 571b1, 655a1, 709a1, 916c1, 1088j1, 1132a1) and one of rank 3 (5077a1), in about 700 non-trivial checks at prime levels m, with no failures; the curves were chosen to separate the roles of the Tamagawa numbers, the real components and the torsion, and an independent re-implementation with the constant predicted in advance confirmed the identity on seven further curves (237 non-trivial levels), and once the normalizations (period, sign of the modular symbol, orientation of the pairing, torsion) are made explicit the identity holds exactly in Ota's form, including his sign (−1)^{ν(m)}, assuming the standard normalization of the Artin map (uniformizer ↦ arithmetic Frobenius) used by Tan, which we have not checked in Ota's text; an independent re-implementation with the constant predicted in advance also confirms the identity in rank 1 (eight curves, 533 levels) and in rank 0 (eight curves, 249 levels), the only place where the factor #Ш is tested with #Ш > 1 (571a1, #Ш = 4; 681b1, #Ш = 9); in all, about 1700 non-trivial prime levels on 33 curves of ranks 0–3. At composite level S = m₁m₂ we verify Ota's inclusion–exclusion form of the conjecture for 389a1, including the mixed coefficient and the sign (−1)^{ν(S)}. The pairing side is computed by an elementary recipe: for R = c\_Tam·#E(F\_m)·P in the formal group at m, the diagonal value ⟨R,R⟩\_m is the class of t(R)/d(R) in F\_mˣ/±1, where t = −x/y and d² is the denominator of x(R). We derive this recipe from the biextension definition of the pairing, assuming the Néron-model description of the canonical local splittings (as in Tan 1995; we have not had access to Mazur–Tate 1983), obtaining the closed formula ⟨R,S⟩ = \[γ/A(R+S)\] with γ the constant term of the primitive line through R and S and A the numerator of the x-coordinate; in particular ⟨R,R⟩ = \[A(R)\]⁻¹. One elementary integrality step is left unproved at ℓ = 2 (verified at 349 points on six curves and at 3631 further points on 453 curves). We know of no earlier numerical verification of the refined conjecture in rank 3, nor of one in rank 2 with a closed formula for the pairing in terms of the coordinates of the points themselves; Bley–Macías Castillo (2022) verified the equivalent 3-adic eTNC statement for 389a1, 433a1 and 446d1 at a handful of primes via Selmer groups and Hilbert symbols. As a secondary result we verify, in 20 cases over three curves and p ∈ {3,5}, that the Kolyvagin derived class κ\_ℓ attached to a rank-2 curve and a Heegner field spans exactly the line ker(E(Q)/p → E(F\_ℓ)/p), with an explicit nonvanishing criterion — a statement implicit in Howard and Mazur–Rubin and stated in an unpublished draft hosted on W. Stein's website; the nonvanishing part was tested in two cases by Jetchev–Lauter–Stein (2009), with which we agree, and the direction had not been tested. All computations are in PARI/GP 2.17 and are reproducible from the accompanying code.

## 1. Setup and notation

Throughout, E/Q is an elliptic curve of conductor N given by a minimal Weierstrass model, with Mordell–Weil group E(Q) of rank r and a fixed basis P₁, …, Pᵣ of E(Q)/E(Q)tors (saturated). We write c\_Tam = ∏\_{ℓ|N} cₗ for the product of Tamagawa numbers and c∞ ∈ {1, 2} for the number of connected components of E(R).

**Modular symbols.** Let x⁺ be the plus modular symbol of E, normalized as in PARI's `msfromell`: for a rational cusp a/m, \[a/m\]⁺ := x⁺({a/m} − {∞}) ∈ Q is the real part of 2πi∫ from a/m to i∞ of f(z)dz, divided by ω₁, the least positive real period of the Néron lattice (the real period over the connected component of E(R)). For the curves below, \[a/m\]⁺ ∈ Z when c∞ = 2 and \[a/m\]⁺ ∈ ½Z when c∞ = 1.

**Theta elements.** For a prime m ∤ N let G\_m = (Z/m)ˣ and G\_m⁺ = G\_m/±1, cyclic of order n := (m−1)/2. Since \[−a/m\]⁺ = \[a/m\]⁺, the Mazur–Tate element

```latex
\theta_m \;=\; \sum_{a \in (\mathbb{Z}/m)^\times} [a/m]^+ \,\sigma_a \;\in\; \mathbb{Q}[G_m^+]
```

is well defined. Fixing a primitive root g mod m and writing σ for the image of σ\_g, we identify Z\[G\_m⁺\] = Z\[σ\]/(σⁿ − 1) and set x = σ − 1, so the augmentation ideal is I = (x). We expand θ\_m = Σ\_k a\_k x^k with a\_k = Σ\_t c\_t C(t, k), where c\_t is the coefficient of σ^t. The relation (1+x)ⁿ = 1 gives n x ≡ −C(n,2) x² − C(n,3) x³ − … (mod Iⁿ), so for k ≥ 1 one has I^k/I^{k+1} ≅ Z/n, and the class of θ\_m in Iʳ/Iʳ⁺¹, assuming θ\_m ∈ Iʳ, is obtained from (a\_0, …, a\_r) by r successive reductions: if a\_j = n·k\_j, replace a\_{j−1+i} by a\_{j−1+i} − k\_j C(n,i) for i ≥ 2. We write c\_r(m) ∈ Z/n for the resulting class. The condition "θ\_m ∈ Iʳ" is exactly that a\_0 ≡ 0 and that each intermediate reduced coefficient is ≡ 0 (mod n); that θ\_m ∈ Iʳ (the "order of vanishing" half of the conjecture) is a theorem of Ota (2018, Thm. 1.2) for the Z\_p-parts with p ≥ 5 (2 and 3 inverted), E without complex multiplication and further hypotheses; our verification is over Z, including the 2- and 3-parts; we verify it in every case below.

**The refined conjecture at prime level.** In the form of Burns–Kurihara–Sano (2021, eqs. (8)–(9)) and Ota (2018, Conj. 2.4) for S = {m}:

```latex
\theta_m \;\equiv\; (-1)^{\nu(m)}\; \#\text{Ш}(E/\mathbb{Q})\; J_m \; R_F \pmod{I^{r+1}},\qquad J_m = \#\operatorname{coker}\Big(E(\mathbb{Q}) \to E(\mathbb{F}_m) \oplus \bigoplus_{\ell | N} E(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)/E_0(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)\Big)
```

where R\_F = det(⟨x\_i, y\_j⟩) is the discriminant of the Mazur–Tate pairing ⟨·,·⟩: E(Q) × E\_S(Q) → G\_m⁺ ≅ I/I², with x\_i a basis of E(Q) and y\_j a basis of the "S-Selmer" sublattice E\_S(Q) = kernel of the map defining J\_m. For the curves considered, #Ш = 1 (analytically) and E(Q)\_tors = 0, except 1088j1 with E(Q)\_tors ≅ Z/2.

## 2. An elementary formula for the Mazur–Tate pairing at a prime level

The Mazur–Tate pairing is defined idèlically via a biextension (Mazur–Tate 1983, 1987; see also Tan 1995, Def. 2.6–2.8). Its definition does not yield a closed formula; earlier numerical work computed it by other means (Portillo-Bobadilla 2019 in rank 1; Séguin 2014 in Darmon's Heegner-point setting; Bley–Macías Castillo 2022 via Selmer groups). We use the following recipe, suggested by the analogy with the Mazur–Stein–Tate formula h\_p(P) = (1/p)·log\_p(σ\_p(P)/d(P)) for cyclotomic p-adic heights:

**Recipe.** Let R ∈ E(Q) lie in the formal group E₁(Q\_m), write x(R) = a/d² in lowest terms with d > 0, and let t(R) = −x(R)/y(R) be the formal parameter. Then v\_m(t) = v\_m(d) ≥ 1, the ratio t(R)/d(R) is an m-adic unit, and we set

```latex
h(R) \;:=\; \log_g \Big[\, t(R)/d(R) \bmod m \,\Big] \;\in\; \mathbb{Z}/n, \qquad \langle R, R\rangle_m \;:=\; 2\,h(R) \;=\; \log_g\big[(t/d)^2\big] \;=\; -\log_g\big[A(R)\big],
```

where x(R) = A(R)/d² and the last equality uses B² ≡ A³ (mod m) for y(R) = B/d³, since t/d = −A/B exactly. We call h the *half-pairing*: it is a quadratic form on E\_S(Q) and the pairing is its polarization, ⟨R₁,R₂⟩ := h(R₁+R₂) − h(R₁) − h(R₂), which gives back 2h(R) on the diagonal; no division by 2 is ever needed. The pairing matrix of a basis R₁, …, R\_r of a sublattice is H := (⟨R\_i,R\_j⟩), with H\_ii = 2h(R\_i). The m-adic sigma function does not appear because σ\_m(t) = t·(1 + O(t)) and t ≡ 0 (mod m), so σ\_m(R)/d(R) ≡ t(R)/d(R) (mod m). The justification of these formulas is in the paragraph "The local computation" below.

**Points in the formal group.** For an arbitrary basis P\_i of E(Q) we take R\_i = M·P\_i with M = c\_Tam·#E(F\_m). Multiplication by #E(F\_m) kills the reduction mod m, and multiplication by c\_Tam puts the point into E\_0(Q\_ℓ) at every bad ℓ, so R\_i lies in the lattice E\_S(Q) of §1 (this is the only place where the Tamagawa numbers enter, and §4 shows that omitting c\_Tam breaks the identity for 446d1, the one curve on which the omission was tested). The coordinates of R\_i are large rationals (roughly M²·ĥ(P\_i)/log 10 digits) but PARI handles them exactly for m up to a few thousand.

**Status of the recipe: a consequence of Tan's definition, up to one local computation.** We have not had access to Mazur–Tate (1983, 1987), but Tan (1995, §2) gives the definition in a form that can be compared with the recipe. Tan's pairing E(K) × E\_S(K) → G is the composite of a pairing with values in C\_S = ∏ C\_v (Def. 2.6) with the Artin map C\_S → G (Def. 2.8), where the local groups are: at a tamely ramified place v ∈ S, A\_v = E(K\_v), B\_v = E₁'(K\_v) (points with trivial reduction) and C\_v = K\_vˣ/(1 + π\_v O\_v) ≅ Z × k\_vˣ; at an unramified non-archimedean v, B\_v = E₀(K\_v) and C\_v = K\_vˣ/O\_vˣ ≅ Z, the pairing being the Néron local symbol; at a real place unramified in L/K, C\_v = 1. For K = Q, S = {m}, L = Q(μ\_m)⁺ this says three things. (i) The domain of the second argument is exactly the lattice E\_S(Q) = {R : R ∈ E₁(Q\_m), R ∈ E₀(Q\_ℓ) for ℓ | N} that the recipe requires — the hypotheses of the recipe are the hypotheses of the definition. (ii) The Artin map kills the uniformizer m at the place m and the subgroup 1 + mZ\_m, so only the unit part of the local value survives, as a class in F\_mˣ/±1 = G\_m⁺; and at ℓ ≠ m it sends ℓᵏ to Frobₗᵏ = \[ℓᵏ\]. Hence ⟨R,R⟩ = Art(ψₘ(p)) · ∏ₗ \[ℓ\]^ψₗ(p) for any point p of the biextension over (R,R), the product being independent of p because the global units are killed (φ(Qˣ) = 0). (iii) The splitting ψₘ is the unique one on the formal group (Hom(E₁, Hom(E₁, Cₘ)) = 0 since E₁ is pro-m and Cₘ has no m-torsion), so it coincides with the reduction mod (1 + mZₘ) of the Mazur–Tate m-adic σ-function, which is t·(1 + O(t)); and the Néron symbols at ℓ | d sum to ordₗ of the denominator. The recipe t/d is precisely this product, read as the quadratic form whose polarization is the pairing; t/d is the half-pairing h, whose polarization is the pairing; the local computation below shows that the pairing itself is \[(t/d)²\] on the diagonal. What remains to make this a proof is the local computation at m and ℓ for one explicit representative p over (R,R), with the Qˣ-ambiguity removed; we have not carried it out. The orientation of the Artin map on local units (u ↦ σ(u) or σ(u⁻¹)) decides whether Tan's pairing is ours or its inverse; we fix the orientation that makes the formulas below hold, and §4(b) shows from the data that Tan's is the inverse.

**The local computation.** We then carried out the computation that the previous paragraph left open; it is short. Realize the Poincaré biextension of E × E by G\_m in the usual way: a point of P over (a, b) is a non-zero rational function f on E with divisor (a+b) + (O) − (a) − (b), and the G\_m-action is scaling. For a = R, b = S in E\_S(Q) take

f = (x − x(R+S)) / ℓ(x, y),  ℓ = the line through R and S (the tangent line if R = S),

which has the right divisor, and scale f by a rational number so that both the numerator d(R+S)²x − A(R+S) and the denominator ℓ₀ = αy + βx + γ (α, β, γ ∈ Z, gcd = 1) are primitive integral forms. Here x(R+S) = A(R+S)/d(R+S)² in lowest terms. The canonical local splittings of Mazur–Tate, in the description through the Néron model, are: at an unramified place ℓ (B\_ℓ = E₀(Q\_ℓ), C\_ℓ = Z) the order of f along the identity component of the special fibre — which is 0 for a primitive f — and at the tamely ramified place m (B\_m = E₁(Q\_m), C\_m = Q\_mˣ/(1+mZ\_m)) the m-adic order together with the reduction of f to the special fibre: since R, S, R+S all reduce to O, the reduction f̄ has trivial divisor and is a non-zero constant in F\_mˣ, and this constant is ψ\_m(f). The reduction is immediate from the primitive forms: d(R+S) ≡ 0, so the numerator reduces to −A(R+S); and since R, S ∈ E₁(Q\_m) the coefficients α, β of the primitive line are divisible by m (the line through two points of E₁ has slope and intercept with m-adic poles of order ≥ 1 and ≥ 3, so after clearing denominators the constant term is the unit), and the denominator reduces to the constant γ. Hence ψ\_m(f) = −A(R+S)/γ ∈ F\_mˣ, all other components are trivial, and by Artin reciprocity

⟨R, S⟩ = \[γ / A(R+S)\] ∈ F\_mˣ/±1 = G\_m⁺,

where we fix the orientation of the Artin map on local units so that this holds (the other orientation inverts every value). This is a closed formula for the Mazur–Tate pairing, valid for all R, S ∈ E\_S(Q) with R, S, R+S ≠ O. It remains to compare it with the recipe. For R = S, writing x(R) = A/d², y(R) = B/d³, the tangent-line constant is γ = (BW − ΛA)/c\_L with W = 2B + a₁Ad + a₃d³, Λ = 3A² + 2a₂Ad² + a₄d⁴ − a₁Bd and c\_L the content of the line; modulo m (d ≡ 0, and B² ≡ A³ from the Weierstrass equation) this is γ ≡ −A³/c\_L, while A(2R) ≡ A⁴/δ², where δ² := N₄/A(2R) is the cancellation in the duplication formula x(2R) = N₄/(d²W²), N₄ := A⁴ − b₄A²d⁴ − 2b₆Ad⁶ − b₈d⁸ (δ is an integer). So ⟨R,R⟩ = \[−δ²/(c\_L·A)\], and the recipe gives ⟨R,R⟩ = 2h(R) = \[(t/d)²\] = \[A/B\]² = \[A\]⁻¹ (because t/d = −A/B exactly and B² ≡ A³). The two agree if and only if δ² ≡ ±c\_L (mod m). The content c\_L is always 1: since BW − ΛA ≡ −A³ (mod d) is coprime to d, c\_L = gcd(W, Λ), and a prime ℓ divides both W = d³·∂F/∂y(R) and Λ = −d⁴·∂F/∂x(R) (F the Weierstrass equation) only if R̄ is a singular point of the reduction, which R ∈ E₀(Qₗ) excludes. For δ at an odd prime ℓ (good or bad) with R ∈ E₀(Qₗ): ℓ | W means R̄ is a non-singular 2-torsion point of the reduction, where 2y + a₁x + a₃ is a uniformizer, so a point at distance j from the 2-torsion has double at distance exactly j from O (the formal group law is \[2\]s = 2s + … with 2 a unit), which forces vₗ(N₄) = 0, i.e. ℓ ∤ δ. At ℓ = 2 the same argument does not apply: it uses that doubling preserves the distance to O, i.e. \[2\]s = 2s + … with 2 a unit, and 2 is not a unit in the formal group at ℓ = 2; we have not written out another argument. Numerically (chkg2.gp), δ = c\_L = 1 and γ/A(2R) ≡ ±(t/d)² at all 349 points R\_i = M·P\_i with 7 ≤ m ≤ 113 on six curves (389a1, 446d1, 1088j1, 916c1, 655a1, 5077a1), and in a separate sweep (gsweep.py) δ = c\_L = 1 at all 3631 points c\_Tam·k·P, 1 ≤ k ≤ 8, P running over the generators, on the 453 curves of positive rank and conductor ≤ 400, which include every reduction type at 2. For points outside E₀(Q₂) on 446d1 and 1088j1 one finds δ = c\_L = 2 and 8, so the hypothesis R ∈ E₀ is used. For R ≠ S the same computation with the chord gives ⟨R,S⟩ = \[γ\_{R,S}/A(R+S)\], and at 129 pairs (R₁, R₂) over five curves this equals \[(t/d)(R+S)/((t/d)(R)(t/d)(S))\], i.e. exactly the polarization h(R+S) − h(R) − h(S) of the recipe — with no 2-torsion ambiguity, which the polarization of a quadratic form alone would leave when n is even. Scripts: `biext.gp`, `chkg2.gp`, `chord.gp`, `gsweep.py`.

In summary: granting the Néron-model description of the canonical local splittings (which is how Mazur–Tate 1983 construct them and how Tan 1995 uses them), the Mazur–Tate pairing on E\_S(Q) × E\_S(Q) at prime level is given exactly by the recipe of this section, the only unproved step being the elementary statement δ = 1 at ℓ = 2 for points of E₀(Q₂), verified in all cases at hand. The diagonal takes the memorable form ⟨R,R⟩ = \[A(R)\]^{∓1}: the Mazur–Tate self-pairing of a point of E\_S(Q) is the class of the numerator of its x-coordinate.

## 3. The identity tested

Let I\_m := \[E(Q) : E\_S(Q)\] be the size of the image of E(Q) in E(F\_m) ⊕ ⊕\_{ℓ|N} E(Q\_ℓ)/E\_0(Q\_ℓ), so that J\_m = M/I\_m with M = c\_Tam·#E(F\_m) as above. Since the pairing is bilinear with E\_S(Q) in one argument, ⟨x\_i, y\_j⟩ = M^{−1}⟨M x\_i, y\_j⟩, and since M·E(Q) ⊂ E\_S(Q) has index Mʳ/I\_m, a change of basis gives

```latex
J_m \, R_F \;=\; \frac{M}{I_m}\cdot \frac{I_m}{M^{2r}}\cdot \det\big(\langle R_i,R_j\rangle_m\big) \;=\; \frac{\det\big(\langle R_i,R_j\rangle_m\big)}{M^{2r-1}}, \qquad R_i = M P_i .
```

The index I\_m therefore cancels, and the prediction of §1 becomes, for #Ш = 1 and up to a constant u\_E depending only on normalizations,

```latex
\boxed{\; M^{2r-1}\; c_r(m) \;\equiv\; u_E \cdot \det_{r\times r}\big(\langle R_i,R_j\rangle_m\big) \pmod{n},\qquad n = (m-1)/2 \;}
```

In practice we test 2ʳ M^{2r−1} c\_r(m) ≡ u\_E·det H (mod n) with H = (⟨R\_i,R\_j⟩) the pairing matrix of §2; the factor 2ʳ on the left is a historical artefact of an earlier normalization and is simply absorbed into u\_E (so u\_E = 2ʳ × the constant of the boxed identity). Both sides depend on the primitive root g: replacing g by g^k multiplies c\_r by k^{−r} and det H by k^{−r}, so the identity is independent of g. The constant u\_E must be the same for all m; we determine it from the first few primes and then test it on all others. The test is non-trivial at a prime m when (lhs, rhs) ≠ (0, 0); when gcd(M, n) > 1 the identity constrains c\_r(m) only modulo n/gcd, and we flag these as weaker checks.

A first version of this computation used the factor N\_m/I\_m² (N\_m := #E(F\_m)) instead of M^{−3} for r = 2; the two agree modulo 3 whenever 3 ∤ N\_m (because N\_m⁴ ≡ 1 mod 3) but not modulo n, and the mod-3 test passed without revealing the error. We mention this because it illustrates why the full-level test is the one that carries information.

"Non-trivial" excludes levels where both sides vanish (15 of the 236 levels for 389a1, for instance). Levels dividing N are excluded. The curves 655a1, 1132a1, 916c1 and 1088j1 were chosen, after a first draft of this note, to separate the three invariants that could enter the constant: c\_∞ = 1 with c\_Tam = 2 and 3 (655a1, 1132a1), c\_∞ = 2 with odd c\_Tam (916c1), and non-trivial torsion (1088j1). The result is that u\_E depends on c\_∞ and on the torsion, and not at all on the Tamagawa numbers (which are fully absorbed by the factor c\_Tam in M):

**u\_E = (−2)ʳ · c\_∞ / #E(Q)\_tors²** in all ten cases (8, 8, 8, 4, −16, 4, 4, 8, 2, 4).

Every factor, including the sign, is accounted for. (a) 2ʳ is the factor we put on the left-hand side of the test (§3) and cancels; in terms of the pairing matrix H the identity is M^{2r−1} c\_r(m) ≡ (−1)ʳ · (c∞ / #E(Q)tors²) · det H. (b) The sign (−1)ʳ — + for the rank-2 curves, − for the rank-3 curve, and − for rank 1 (eight curves, 533 levels, in the independent re-implementation of §4 below) — is the product of two conventions. First, the pairing: Tan's Artin map (his §1, Weil group W\_D = Kˣ\\Aˣ/U\_D) sends a uniformizer π\_v to the Frobenius \[v\]; by global reciprocity a local unit u at m then corresponds to σ\_u⁻¹ (choose a ∈ Z with a ≡ u mod m; the principal idèle a gives ∏\_{ℓ|a} Frob\_ℓ^{v\_ℓ(a)} = σ\_a, so \[u at m\] = σ\_a⁻¹), whereas our recipe uses u ↦ σ\_u. So Tan's pairing is the inverse of ours, P := −H, i.e. ⟨R,R⟩ = \[A(R)\] rather than \[A(R)\]⁻¹, and det P = (−1)ʳ det H, which removes the (−1)ʳ. Second, the theta element: PARI's plus symbol satisfies x⁺({0} − {∞}) = −L(E,1)/ω₁ (checked on seven rank-0 curves of both discriminant signs), whereas Mazur–Tate normalize \[0\]⁺ = +L(E,1)/Ω⁺; so θ\_m of §1 is −c∞ times the Mazur–Tate element θ\_m⁺, and this global −1 is exactly Ota's (−1)^{ν(m)} = −1 at prime level — provided Ota's Artin map is normalized as Tan's (uniformizer ↦ arithmetic Frobenius), which is the standard choice but which we have not checked in his text; this is question 3 of our letter to him. An earlier draft of this paragraph said the discrepancy could not be removed by an orientation change; that was wrong because it took the sign of θ\_m for granted, and the rank-1 data (which force a factor (−1)^{r+1} = −(−1)ʳ, not (−1)^{r(r−1)/2}) exposed it. The composite-level test below confirms the relative sign (−1)^{ν(S)−ν(m)} as well. (c) The symbols x⁺ of PARI are normalized by the least real period ω₁ of the Néron lattice, which is the full real period when c∞ = 1 but only half of it when c∞ = 2; and θ\_m as defined in §1 is 2·Σ \[a/m\]⁺ σ\_a over a ∈ G\_m⁺, because both a and −a are summed. If instead the symbols are normalized by Ω⁺\_int := the generator of the real projection of the lattice — which is ω₁ when c∞ = 2 and ω₁/2 when c∞ = 1, and is the normalization that makes \[a/m\]⁺ an integer in all cases — and θ\_m⁺ := −Σ \[a/m\]⁺ σ\_a over a ∈ G\_m⁺ (the sign from (b)), then θ\_m = −c∞ · θ\_m⁺ and the factor c∞ disappears. (d) The factor 1/#E(Q)tors² is the torsion normalization of the discriminant in Tan's Def. 2.13 (disc\_S = |A\_tors|⁻¹|B\_tors|⁻¹ det) combined with the torsion contribution to the index \[E(Q) : E\_S(Q)\] in J\_m; it is the same tors² as in the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer formula and in Burns–Kurihara–Sano's eq. (9). With these normalizations the identity reads, for all curves and ranks 0–3 (rank 0: eight curves and 249 levels in the re-implementation, including #Ш = 4 and 9; ranks 1–3: this note and the re-implementation),

θ\_m⁺ ≡ (−1)^{ν(m)} · #Ш · J\_m · disc\_m(P) = − #Ш · det P / (M^{2r−1} · #E(Q)tors²) (mod I^{r+1}),  with P = (⟨R\_i,R\_j⟩) in Tan's orientation, ⟨R,R⟩ = \[A(R)\] (and #Ш = 1 for every curve of positive rank here),

which is Ota's Conj. 2.4 at prime level with no undetermined constant. The value u\_E = 4 for 571b1 supersedes the value 2 quoted in an earlier draft, which we could not reproduce.

**The Tamagawa test (446d1).** With R\_i = #E(F\_m)·P\_i, i.e. omitting c\_Tam = 2, the identity fails at 28 levels of a run over 5 ≤ m ≤ 400. With R\_i = 2·#E(F\_m)·P\_i, so that R\_i ∈ E\_0(Q₂) as the definition of E\_S(Q) requires, it holds at 61 of 61 non-trivial levels with the same u = 8 as for the other c\_∞ = 2 curves. This is the definition of J\_m through the joint cokernel in Burns–Kurihara–Sano being confirmed in detail.

**The vanishing levels.** For 389a1 at m ≡ 1 (mod 9) the mod-3 reduction of the identity was examined separately: the levels at which ord\_I θ\_m jumps from 2 to 4 (m = 37, 163, 613, 757, 829, 937 among m ≤ 1200) are exactly the levels at which det⟨R\_i,R\_j⟩ ≡ 0 (mod 3). The frequency of such jumps (6 of the 20 levels m ≡ 1 (mod 9) in the mod-3 run, m ≤ 1200) matches the probability that a random symmetric 2×2 matrix over F₃ is singular (9/27). Jumps are always by an even amount (ord ∈ {2, 4, 6}), consistent with the alternating character of the second derived pairing (Bertolini–Darmon 1994, Thm. 3.18). At levels with 3 | #E(F\_m), where the mod-3 identity is not directly applicable, the implication "leading term ≢ 0 mod p ⇒ p ∤ J\_m" of Ota's theorem — which is proved only for p ≥ 5 and under further hypotheses, so that at p = 3 it is a consistency check and not a theorem — would force c₂ ≡ 0 (mod 3) at m = 127, 487, 919, which is what occurs. The re-implementation checked the same implication at p = 5 and 7 on eight curves (m ≤ 3000; m ≤ 10000 for 389a1 and 709a1): 11 levels with p | J\_m (8 at p = 5, 3 at p = 7), no violations. Nine of them satisfy all the hypotheses of Ota's theorem that we could check (no CM; p ∤ 6N·#E(F\_p)·∏c\_ℓ; E(F\_m)\[p\] cyclic; ρ̄\_p surjective, by Serre's criterion for semistable curves and the absence of a rational p-isogeny); the other two (389a1 at m = 1831, where E(F\_m)\[5\] is not cyclic, and 997b1 at p = 5, where 5 | #E(F\_5)) are outside its range. No level with p ≥ 11 dividing J\_m occurs in this range; such levels are structurally rare (p | (m−1)/2, p | #E(F\_m) and non-surjectivity of E(Q) → E(F\_m)/p at once).

**Rank 3.** For 5077a1 the class c₃(m) ∈ I³/I⁴ and the 3×3 determinant agree with u = −16 at all 49 non-trivial levels tested. The order-of-vanishing statement θ\_m ∈ I³ alone (tested mod 3 on 8 further levels m ≡ 1 mod 9) showed the leading coefficient vanishing mod 3 more often than in rank 2 (6 of 8), which the full-level computation explains as degeneracy of the 3×3 pairing mod 3 rather than any defect in the conjecture.

**Composite levels (Ota's inclusion–exclusion).** For S = m₁m₂ the conjecture (Ota, Conj. 2.4, which refers to Mazur–Tate 1987, (2.5)–(2.6), (3.1) for d\_T and μ\_{S,T}; we reconstructed μ\_{S,T} from the norm relation rather than from that paper) reads, for θ̃\_S the image of θ\_S in Z\[G\_S⁺\] (Ota's notation), θ̃\_S ≡ #Ш · Σ\_{T | S} (−1)^{ν(T)} J\_T η\_r(μ\_{S,T}(d\_T)) in I\_Sʳ/I\_Sʳ⁺¹, where d\_T is the discriminant of the level-T pairing and μ\_{S,T} lifts Symʳ(G\_T⁺) to Symʳ(G\_S⁺) and multiplies by φ(S/T). We push everything to the quotient Ḡ = Z/n₁ × Z/n₂ of G\_S⁺ (n\_i = (m\_i − 1)/2), where I²/I³ ≅ Z/n₁·x₁² ⊕ Z/g·x₁x₂ ⊕ Z/n₂·x₂² with g = gcd(n₁, n₂) (Passi's theorem I²/I³ ≅ Sym²(Ḡ)), and the level-S pairing of R ∈ E\_S(Q) is the pair of level-m\_i recipes (h¹(R), h²(R)). Writing Hᵏ for the polarized matrix at m\_k and R\_i = c\_Tam·#E(F\_{m₁})·#E(F\_{m₂})·P\_i, the discriminant is det(H¹x₁ + H²x₂) = D₁x₁² + D₁₂x₁x₂ + D₂x₂² with D\_k = det Hᵏ and D₁₂ = H¹₁₁H²₂₂ + H²₁₁H¹₂₂ − 2H¹₁₂H²₁₂. Two consequences, in rank 2: the terms T = m₁, m₂ contribute only to x\_i², where the sum over T collapses (by the norm relation π\_{S/m₂}θ\_S = (a\_{m₂} − σ − σ⁻¹)θ\_{m₁}, which is a theorem) to the statement that the x₁²-coefficient of θ\_S equals (a\_{m₂} − 2)·c₂(m₁) mod n₁; and the mixed coefficient receives only the term T = S, giving the one genuinely new prediction (a₁₁ denotes the coefficient of x₁x₂ in the expansion of θ\_S, after the reductions of §1 in each variable)

4 M³ · a₁₁(θ\_S) ≡ −u\_E · D₁₂ (mod g),  M = c\_Tam·#E(F\_{m₁})·#E(F\_{m₂}),

with the same u\_E = 8 as at prime level and the sign reversed, because ν(S) = 2. For 389a1 we tested all 28 pairs of primes 7 ≤ m₁ < m₂ ≤ 61 with g ≥ 3 (code `mt_ota.gp`, `mt_ota_run.py`): θ\_S ∈ I\_S² in every case; the x₁² and x₂² norm-relation checks hold in every case; and the mixed identity holds in every case, non-trivially (both sides ≠ 0) at 10 pairs with g ∈ {3, 5, 6, 7}, including (11, 31) mod 5 and (29, 43) mod 7, where the alternatives u = −8 (no sign change) and u = ±4, ±16 are excluded. The remaining pairs have D₁₂ ≡ 0 ≡ a₁₁ (mod g) and are consistent but uninformative. The cost is in the points, not the symbols: R\_i has about (m₁m₂)²·ĥ(P\_i) nats, i.e. 10⁵–10⁶ digits for m\_i ≤ 61, so m\_i of a few hundred are out of reach of exact arithmetic without a p-adic shortcut. An earlier draft of this paragraph, written before the formula was implemented, said that the three second-order coefficients "do not follow a single-constant extrapolation of the prime-level identity"; that was correct and is explained by the T = m\_i terms above.

**Comparison with Bley–Macías Castillo at their levels.** Bley and Macías Castillo (2022, §4.4) verify the 3-part of the equivariant Tamagawa number conjecture for E over the cubic subfield F of Q(ζ\_ℓ) for 389a1 (ℓ = 7, 13, 43), 433a1 (ℓ = 7, 13, 31, 37) and 446d1 (ℓ = 19). The comparison we can make without Magma is therefore at the level of that determinant: in our data, det⟨R\_i, R\_j⟩\_m (mod 3) is non-zero at all eight of their levels (389a1: 2, 1, 2 at m = 7, 13, 43; 433a1: 2, 2, 1, 1 at m = 7, 13, 31, 37; 446d1: 1 at m = 19), in agreement with the nonvanishing their verification certifies, and our full identity mod n = (m−1)/2 holds at each of these levels (433a1, c\_∞ = 1, is the tenth curve in the table, added for this comparison, with u\_E = 4 at 21 levels). The two pairings are related by the projection G\_m⁺ → Gal(F/Q) ≅ Z/3, so this is the expected agreement rather than an independent confirmation of the values; matching Ψ entry by entry would require re-running their code.

## 5. Direction of Kolyvagin classes in rank 2

Let E/Q have rank 2 and root number +1, K an imaginary quadratic field satisfying the Heegner hypothesis for N, and p an odd prime with E\[p\] irreducible, p ∤ N·c\_Tam·#E(K)tors. For a Kolyvagin prime ℓ (inert in K, p | aₗ, p | ℓ+1) let yₗ ∈ E(K\[ℓ\]) be the Heegner point of conductor ℓ and κₗ ∈ H¹(K, E\[p\]) the Kolyvagin derived class of Σ σ Dₗ yₗ, the sum over coset representatives σ of G = Gal(K\[ℓ\]/K\[1\]) ≅ Z/(ℓ+1) in Gal(K\[ℓ\]/K). Since the Heegner point y\_K ∈ E(K) is torsion (here literally 0), κₗ ∈ Sel\_p(E/K)⁺ = E(Q)/pE(Q) ≅ F\_p² when Ш(E/Q)\[p\] = 0. Let Q generate E^D(Q) ⊗ Q, D = disc K; in every case below rank E^D(Q) = 1, checked with ellrank.

**Statement tested.** (a) κ\_ℓ ≠ 0 if and only if the reduction map E(Q)/p → E(F\_ℓ)/pE(F\_ℓ) is nonzero and Q is not p-divisible in E^D(F\_ℓ). (b) When κ\_ℓ ≠ 0, F\_p·κ\_ℓ = ker(E(Q)/p → E(F\_ℓ)/pE(F\_ℓ)); in particular the line is independent of K.

Both follow from the transverse local condition at ℓ in Kolyvagin systems (Howard 2004, Def. 1.2.2; Mazur–Rubin 2004) together with the vanishing of y\_K and the core-rank-one structure theory; the statement appears as Prop. 0.2 of an unpublished and incomplete draft hosted on W. Stein's website (no author or date given; its proof refers to a missing lemma). We are not aware of any numerical test of (b).

**Method.** The h = ℓ+1 (or 2(ℓ+1) when h(K) = 2) Heegner points of conductor ℓ are computed to 150–450 digits from the modular parametrization; x(y\_ℓ) has a rational minimal polynomial f of degree h, recognized exactly (denominators up to 10¹¹³). A reduced primitive element θ = B(x) (`polredbest`) gives L = Q(θ); the Galois action of a generator σ of G on the roots is recovered, without interpolation, from the integer resolvents ∏(T − (θ\_k + c(θ\_{σk} + θ\_{σ⁻¹k}))), c = 1,2,3, and (for orientation) the O\_K-resolvents ∏(T − (θ\_k + cθ\_{σk})), c = 2,3. The y-coordinate is recovered from the exact square root of (4x³ + b₂x² + 2b₄x + b₆)/(4D) in L (`nfroots`), whose existence encodes the action of complex conjugation. At each prime q ≡ 1 (mod p) totally split in K\[ℓ\], all points reduce to E(F\_q), the derivative D\_ℓ = Σ iσⁱ is applied along the σ-cycle of roots mod q, and the class of the result in E(F\_q)/p is read off by the order-p Tate pairing (checked against direct enumeration of pE(F\_q)) and compared with the reductions of P₁, P₂ and Q. Twenty such primes identify the class uniquely; the trace relation Tr y\_ℓ = a\_ℓ y₁ holds at every prime as a check. A control case with a theorem-level prediction (43a1, rank 1, K = Q(√−8), ℓ = 29, where κ = 0 is forced) gave 0 at 15/15 primes.

Twenty cases, three curves, two primes p, five zero predictions, four distinct lines of F\_p², no failures (the 43a1 control is not counted). Basis vectors P₁, P₂ are the saturated `ellrank` generators of each curve (for 389a1 in §5: (−1,1), (0,0) in the first row, `ellrank`'s (0,−1), (1,0) elsewhere; since (0,−1) = −P₂ and (1,0) = P₁ + P₂ in the first basis, P₁ − P₂ ≡ P₂′ − P₁′ (mod 3) and the label ⟨P₁−P₂⟩ denotes the same line in both). For h(K) = 2 the class must be summed over the two cosets of G with consistent orientation; summing one coset gives a well-defined but wrong class, which is how the h = 2 computation first failed.

**Comparison with Jetchev–Lauter–Stein.** Two of the cases above were computed before, by a different method: JLS (2009, Prop. 3.10 and Rem. 3.11) show κ₅ ≠ 0 for 389a1 and for 709a1 with K = Q(√−7), p = 3, by proving that the Heegner point P₅ is not 3-divisible in E(K\[5\]) (the polynomial φ₃(x) − x(P₅)ψ₃(x)² has no root in K\[5\]). Our table gives κ₅ ≠ 0 in both cases, with the additional information of the line: ⟨P₁+P₂⟩ for 389a1 and ⟨P₁⟩ for 709a1. JLS did not compute the direction of the class, and the two methods share no code or intermediate data beyond the Heegner points themselves; their nonvanishing results and ours agree.

## 6. Relation to the literature and what is new

**Theory.** Mazur–Tate (1987) formulated the refined conjectures; we cite it only through Tan, Ota and Burns–Kurihara–Sano, not having had access to it. Ota (2018) proved θ\_S ∈ I^{r} (the order part) for the p-parts under hypotheses, and the implication "leading term ≠ 0 mod p ⇒ Ш\[p\] = 0 and p ∤ J", both for p ≥ 5 (his §§3, 5). Burns–Kurihara–Sano (2021, published IMRN 2025) recast the leading-term conjecture in the form used here and proved cases in rank 1 (Cor. 1.2: supersingular reduction, squarefree conductor and further conditions, the leading term up to an automorphism of Gal(F/Q)); they also remark (§1) that the only supporting numerical evidence then available was for r = 1 (Portillo-Bobadilla); Bullach–Honnor (2025) prove substantial parts of Mazur–Tate's Conjectures 3, 4 and 6 for p > 3 under a big-image hypothesis (e.g. E semistable and p ≥ 11) and mild local conditions; their leading-term result is trivial in positive rank (their Rem. 1.8(b)). Bertolini–Darmon (1994, 1995) introduced derived heights for the degenerate case; the parity phenomenon in §4 is consistent with their Thm. 3.18.

**Numerics.** Portillo-Bobadilla (2019) treats rank 1. Séguin (McGill M.Sc. thesis, 2014/15) tests Darmon's Heegner-point analogue of the refined conjecture, in conductor 37 over real quadratic fields, computing Mazur–Tate pairings in that setting; it is not a test of the Mazur–Tate conjectures over Q. Llerena-Córdova (2024) tests three of Mazur–Tate's conjectures on 425,713 pairs (E, p) with split multiplicative reduction at p, finding failures that disappear when a factor 1/#E(Q)\_tors is included; it does not treat the rank ≥ 2 leading-term statement with a Mazur–Tate regulator (its Conj. 2.12 asserts only the order of vanishing in positive rank); its conclusion that 1/#E(Q)\_tors must be adjoined is consistent with the factor 1/#E(Q)\_tors² in u\_E found here. The closest work is Bley–Macías Castillo (2022): a numerical verification, assuming classical BSD for E/F and Ш\[3\] = 0, of the 3-adic equivariant Tamagawa number conjecture — equivalent to congruences in the augmentation filtration involving the Mazur–Tate pairing — for 389a1 (ℓ = 7, 13, 43), 433a1 and 446d1 (ℓ = 19), in the cubic subfield of Q(ζ\_ℓ), with the pairing computed via generalized Selmer groups and Hilbert symbols in Magma.

**What is new here**, to the best of our knowledge: (i) a verification of the leading-term conjecture in rank 3; (ii) in rank 2, a verification with θ\_m computed from modular symbols at the full level G\_m⁺ (moduli (m−1)/2 up to 749), with both sides independent, at 21–221 non-trivial levels per curve; (iii) the elementary coordinate formula for the pairing of §2 — ⟨R,S⟩ = \[γ/A(R+S)\], ⟨R,R⟩ = \[A(R)\]⁻¹ — with its derivation from the biextension, which we have not found stated anywhere; (iv) the explicit confirmation that the Tamagawa factor enters through the joint kernel defining E\_S(Q); (v) the direction test for Kolyvagin classes of §5. Items (i)–(iv) are verifications of an existing conjecture, not new mathematics; item (iii) is a formula whose proof is complete except for the two points named in §7.1.

## 7. Caveats, open points and next steps

1. **Status of the pairing formula.** §2 derives the recipe from the biextension definition, granting the Néron-model description of the canonical local splittings (vertical order at unramified places; m-adic order and reduction to the special fibre at m). What is still not written out is an elementary integrality statement: that the cancellation δ in the duplication formula is 1 at ℓ = 2 for points of E₀(Q₂). It is proved for all odd ℓ (and the content of the line is proved to be 1 at every ℓ); the argument fails at ℓ = 2 because it uses that 2 is a unit in the formal group. It holds at the 349 + 3631 points checked, which cover every reduction type at 2 systematically: 166 curves of good reduction at 2, multiplicative I\_n with n ≤ 19, and all additive types II, III, IV, I₀\*, I\_n\*, II\*, III\*, IV\* (count in the companion repository, kodaira2.py). We do not attempt to force an argument at ℓ = 2 here. Someone with Mazur–Tate (1983) in hand should also confirm that the splittings we used are theirs; Tan's description of (L3)/(L4) and the uniqueness argument of §2 (iii) leave little room for a different choice. With these two points settled the formula ⟨R,S⟩ = \[γ\_{R,S}/A(R+S)\], and hence ⟨R,R⟩ = \[A(R)\]⁻¹, is a theorem.
2. **Powers of 2 and sign — explained.** The constant u\_E = (−2)ʳ·c∞/#E(Q)tors² of §4 is explained factor by factor, sign included, and the stress tests on 655a1, 1132a1, 916c1 and 1088j1 confirm that the Tamagawa numbers do not enter it. Our conventions are: x = σ − 1 (so ηᵣ(g₁⋯gᵣ) = ∏(σ(gᵢ) − 1), as in Ota and Burns–Kurihara–Sano), the primitive root g fixed by PARI's znprimroot, PARI's plus symbol (which has x⁺({0}−{∞}) = −L(E,1)/ω₁, the opposite sign to Mazur–Tate's \[0\]⁺ = L(E,1)/Ω⁺), and the pairing ⟨R,S⟩ = \[γ/A(R+S)\] of §2, whose inverse is the pairing in Tan's orientation. Undoing these two sign conventions turns our (−1)ʳ into Ota's (−1)^{ν(m)} = −1 for every r, as §4(b) explains, assuming that Ota normalizes the Artin map as Tan does (uniformizer ↦ arithmetic Frobenius; the standard choice, not checked in his text and asked of him directly); the rank-1 data of the independent re-implementation (533 levels, u\_E = −2c∞) were what fixed the dependence on r. The v0.1 text of this item said the sign was open and suggested a factor (−1)^{r(r−1)/2}; both statements were wrong.
3. **Weak levels.** When gcd(M, n) > 1 the identity determines c\_r(m) only modulo n/gcd(M,n). About half the levels for 389a1 are of this kind. They are still non-trivial checks, but the headline counts should be read with this in mind.
4. **One analytic input.** #Ш = 1 is taken from the analytic order (strong BSD), and every curve of rank ≥ 1 tested here (ours and the re-implementation's) has #Ш = 1, so the factor #Ш in the conjecture is tested only in rank 0 (by the re-implementation, on 571a1 with Ш = 4 and 681b1 with Ш = 9; Llerena-Córdova's rank-0 tests cover it on a much larger scale at primes of split multiplicative reduction). The identity tested in positive rank is therefore a joint consistency check of Mazur–Tate and of BSD's Ш-term; a curve with Ш ≠ 1 in rank 2 would be a sharper test (none exists in Cremona's tables up to conductor 500,000: the analytic-Ш column of the allbsd files is 1 for all 279,056 optimal rank-2 curves, checked with an awk one-liner recorded in the companion document).
5. **Composite levels** are now tested through Ota's inclusion–exclusion formula for S = m₁m₂ (§4); the mixed coefficient x₁x₂ is the only genuinely new information at composite level and is checked only modulo gcd(n₁, n₂), which limits the strength of each individual check; extending to three prime factors and to larger gcd is straightforward but costly (the points R\_i have \~10⁶ digits).
6. **Rank 4** (234446a1, \[1,−1,0,−79,289\], c\_Tam = 2, c\_∞ = 2). The code runs unchanged for r = 4 (the identity is in I⁴/I⁵ with a 4×4 determinant), but PARI's `msfromell` for conductor 234446 needs more than the 7 GB available to us: the attempt was killed by the out-of-memory handler before the first level. On a machine with \~32 GB, or with a modular-symbol implementation that works in the plus quotient only, this is a few hours of computation.
7. **Bley–Macías Castillo.** The comparison in §4 is at the level of det Ψ ≠ 0 (mod 3), which is all their published output allows. An entry-by-entry match of the 2×2 matrix Ψ against the mod-3 reduction of our pairing matrix would be a sharper test; it requires running their Magma code (available on W. Bley's page), to which we have no access.

None of this touches the archimedean Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture: the "derivative" in the Mazur–Tate conjecture is in the group-ring direction σ − 1, not in the complex variable s, and the identification of the two is the open problem.

## Appendix. A worked example: 389a1 at m = 23

E: y² + y = x³ + x² − 2x, P₁ = (−1, 1), P₂ = (0, 0), c\_Tam = 1, c\_∞ = 2, E(Q)\_tors = 0. Take m = 23, so n = 11, g = 5 (PARI's primitive root), G₂₃⁺ = ⟨σ⟩ ≅ Z/11.

**Theta element.** The plus symbols \[a/23\]⁺ for a = 1, …, 22 are

0, −1, 0, 0, −1, −1, −1, 1, 2, 1, 0, 0, 1, 2, 1, −1, −1, −1, 0, 0, −1, 0

(symmetric under a ↔ 23 − a, as they must be). Collecting a and −a by t = log₅(a) mod 11, the coefficient vector of θ₂₃ = Σ c\_t σᵗ is

(c₀, …, c₁₀) = (0, −2, −2, 2, 0, 0, 2, −2, −2, 0, 4).

Expanding σᵗ = (1 + x)ᵗ: a₀ = Σ c\_t = 0, a₁ = Σ t c\_t = 22 = 2·11, a₂ = Σ C(t,2) c\_t = 116. So θ₂₃ ∈ I, and since a₁ = 2n the relation n·x ≡ −C(11,2)x² gives θ₂₃ ∈ I² with leading class c₂(23) = 116 − 2·55 = 6 ∈ Z/11.

**Points.** #E(F₂₃) = 28 (a₂₃ = −4), so M = 28 and R\_i = 28·P\_i. The x-coordinate of R₁ has a 234-digit numerator and denominator d(R₁)² with

d(R₁) = 607852338707617298241916768285023402187465773080649173756581131907387799193727318114990310685196156742789291848564509 (117 digits),

d(R₂) = 16196160423545762519618471188475392072306453021094652577 (56 digits), and d(R₁ + R₂) has 81 digits. In all three cases v₂₃(d) = v₂₃(t) = 1, as expected for points in E₁(Q₂₃) ∖ E₂(Q₂₃). The unit parts give

(t/d)(R₁) ≡ 14, (t/d)(R₂) ≡ 14, (t/d)(R₁ + R₂) ≡ 20 (mod 23),

and with 5¹⁰ ≡ 9 ≡ −14, 5⁵ ≡ 20 (mod 23) the logarithms in Z/11 are h(R₁) = 10, h(R₂) = 10, h(R₁+R₂) = 5. (That h(R₁) = h(R₂) is a coincidence of this level.) The polarized matrix is

H = (2h(R₁), h(R₁+R₂) − h(R₁) − h(R₂); ·, 2h(R₂)) = (20, −15; −15, 20) ≡ (9, 7; 7, 9) (mod 11),  det H ≡ 81 − 49 ≡ 10 (mod 11).

**The identity.** Left side: 2²·M³·c₂ = 4·28³·6; with 28 ≡ 6 and 6³ ≡ 7 (mod 11) this is 4·7·6 = 168 ≡ 3. Right side: u\_E·det H = 8·10 = 80 ≡ 3 (mod 11). The two sides agree, with the same u\_E = 8 = (−2)²·c\_∞ as at the other 220 non-trivial levels for this curve. In the normalization of §4 (θ⁺ = θ/c\_∞ has leading class 6/2 = 3; torsion trivial) this reads M⁻³·det H = 8·10 ≡ 3 ≡ θ₂₃/c\_∞; with the Mazur–Tate sign of the symbol (θ⁺ = −θ/c\_∞, class −3 ≡ 8) and Tan's orientation of the pairing (P = −H, det P = det H), this is θ₂₃⁺ ≡ −J₂₃·disc₂₃(P) in I²/I³ ≅ Z/11, i.e. Ota's statement with (−1)^{ν} = −1.

The whole computation is `thetaclass(E,23,2)` and `pairdet(E,23,[P1,P2],28,11,Mod(5,23))` in `mt_rank.gp`, and takes well under a second.

## 8. Reproducibility, acknowledgements, references

**Code and data.** All computations use PARI/GP 2.17.2 through cypari2 2.2.4, on Cremona's tables (`allbsd`, `allcurves`). The scripts `mt_full.gp` / `mt_full_run.py` (prime-level identity, rank 2), `mt_rank.gp` / `mt_rank_run.py` (general rank), `mt_lead.gp` (mod-3 version), `mt_comp.gp` (exploratory composite levels), mt\_ota.gp / mt\_ota\_run.py (Ota's composite-level formula), biext.gp / chkg2.gp / chord.gp / gsweep.py (the biextension computation of §2 and the integrality sweep), `heeg.gp` + `kgen3.py` (Kolyvagin classes, any curve and p) and the raw logs are in the accompanying archive. A typical prime-level check for 389a1 up to m = 1500 takes about 40 minutes on one core; the Kolyvagin computation for a field of degree 60 takes 15–20 minutes, dominated by `polredbest`. Two PARI conventions matter: `elltatepairing(E, P, Q, m)` takes the m-torsion point as **P**, and its value lies in F\_qˣ/(F\_qˣ)ᵐ and must be raised to (q−1)/m; `ellpadicheightmatrix` returns \[A, B\] with the cyclotomic height matrix equal to A − s₂·B.
